Every so often you meet an apprentice whose progress makes the whole point of the programme obvious. Charlotte Gregson is one of those stories. We spoke to her, and to her line manager at RSM, to understand what it actually looks like when someone builds a marketing function from a standing start, while studying for it at the same time.

## From telesales to the only marketer in the room

Charlotte joined RSM in a telesales role, after a run of hospitality jobs and some time travelling. Marketing wasn't part of the plan until she came across the Multi-Channel Marketing (MCM) apprenticeship while exploring what might come next. She talked it through with her line manager, and they decided together that she should apply.

Charlotte enrolled on our Multi-Channel Marketing apprenticeship alongside the Digital Marketing Institute (DMI) qualification, and stepped into a role that didn't really exist at RSM yet: the business's first and only dedicated marketing professional.

<aside class="de-key-term">
  <strong>No internal expertise to lean on</strong>
  <p>With nobody else in the business doing marketing, Charlotte's classroom sessions and DMI content became her primary source of structured learning and professional development.</p>
</aside>

She put it plainly: *"the classroom sessions and the DMI content were something that I massively relied on to learn how to know what to do in my job."* When there's no colleague to check your thinking against, the course itself has to do that job. For Charlotte, it did.

## What the apprenticeship gave her

<div class="de-chip-list" aria-label="What Charlotte gained from her apprenticeship">
  <span>Marketing theory she could apply immediately</span>
  <span>Confidence to lead initiatives</span>
  <span>Practical skills in Google Ads and analytics</span>
  <span>A qualification that validates her expertise</span>
</div>

That's the short version. The longer version is that Charlotte didn't just learn SEO, copywriting, and campaign planning in a classroom and leave it there. She brought it straight back into a business with no existing marketing muscle to absorb it, which meant every new skill turned into something RSM had never done before.

## Building three brands out of one

Before Charlotte joined, RSM had no structured marketing activity at all. Through her work, that changed:

<section class="de-callout-grid">
  <article>
    <h3>Where RSM started</h3>
    <p>One undefined brand, no campaign planning, and no history of proactive customer engagement.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Where RSM is now</h3>
    <p>Three distinct, clearly positioned brands, each with its own identity and social presence, backed by planned campaigns.</p>
  </article>
</section>

She also introduced campaign planning and execution as a discipline, something the business had never done before. Her summer campaign, and the brand awareness work that followed it, marked a real shift in how RSM approached growth and customer engagement, not just a one-off project.

## Launching RSM's first Google Ads campaign

One of Charlotte's most significant achievements was launching and managing RSM's first Google Ads campaign, from keyword research through to ad creation and optimisation. She led the entire process herself.

<div class="de-strip" aria-label="Campaign results">
  <div>
    <strong>Surge</strong>
    <span>in inbound enquiries, the highest RSM has recorded</span>
  </div>
  <div>
    <strong>5 hrs saved</strong>
    <span>per week through structured planning</span>
  </div>
  <div>
    <strong>2 hrs less</strong>
    <span>supervision needed each week</span>

    <span>in campaign open rates</span>

As Charlotte described it: *"We've had more inbound inquiries in the past few weeks than I think we have ever had."* The campaign was also monitored for return on investment throughout, and it delivered, alongside insights into customer behaviour that helped refine targeting between domestic and commercial audiences.

<p class="de-reflection"><strong>Reflection:</strong> If your organisation's marketing activity started tomorrow with one person and no history to build on, what would you tackle first?</p>

## What it means for Charlotte, and for RSM

For Charlotte, the apprenticeship provided the qualification, the confidence, and the real-world experience she needed to build a marketing career without going to university. As she put it, it *"gave me the confidence and the knowledge that I need to be able to do this successfully."*

For RSM, the impact has been just as concrete. They gained a marketer who has:

- Built marketing processes from scratch
- Delivered measurable commercial results
- Strengthened their brand identity
- Introduced strategic, data-driven marketing practices

Her line manager summed up the change: *"we've seen Charlotte grow in confidence and capability. What once were ideas are now structured plans, carefully thought out, aligned to our business goals, and effectively executed. It's been great to see ideas and tentative plans turn into campaigns and independence."*

Charlotte's journey is a good reminder of what apprenticeships are actually for: not just a qualification at the end, but the structured, applied learning that lets someone build a real capability while they're still building the confidence to use it.

It was a pleasure for us at BPP and Estio to have Charlotte on board, and to share her story. Congrats, Charlotte, and we can't wait to see what's next for you.
